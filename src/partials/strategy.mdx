## Pacing on the day

- **Budget 2.26 minutes per item.** Checkpoints: item 18 by about 40 minutes, item 35 by about 80 minutes, leaving time for flagged items.
- **Flag and move on.** Candidates on the sibling Architect exam report flagging heavily early and finishing with 15 to 20 minutes to revisit. Hard items read easier on the second pass.
- **There is no guessing penalty.** Never leave an item blank. Eliminate what you can, pick, flag.
- **Multiple-response has no partial credit** (assume so; the guide does not promise it). Pick exactly the number asked for.
- The [exam simulator](../exam/) reports your pace against this line after every run.

## How the items are built

Every practice item on this site follows the style of the three published samples:

1. **One buried constraint decides the answer.** "Not needed until morning" means batch. "Must stay in region" means regional endpoints. "Reusable across applications" means an MCP server. "Must happen every time" means a hook. Read the stem once for the scenario and once hunting the constraint.
2. **"Must" or "always" means a deterministic control.** A system-prompt instruction is a request; a hook, permission rule, or schema constraint is a guarantee. When the stem demands a guarantee, the prompt-based option is the distractor.
3. **Distractors are real mechanisms in the wrong place.** Each wrong option is something that works somewhere else. The question is never "does this exist" but "does this fit these constraints".
4. **Single words decide.** "Guaranteed", "fastest", "cheapest", "without code changes", "for every user". Underline them mentally.

## What candidates report

These are themes from public write-ups by people who sat CCDV-F or the sibling Architect exam. They are one-person accounts, not data, and none of them contain exam items. **[Likely]** on the themes, **[Guessing]** on any single claim.

- **A third of the exam is not about Claude.** Domain 2 includes REST semantics, versioning, async, git, code review and refactoring. People who only studied Claude features were caught out.
- **Specifics matter.** Fast mode, extended and adaptive thinking, effort levels, SDK retry behaviour, context management and tool use were called out by a candidate who scored in the 900s.
- **Free practice tests are easier than the real thing.** Learn the reasoning, not answer keys. This site writes items harder on purpose and draws harder items more often in the simulator.
- **Topics the Architect exam excluded are in scope here**: streaming, vision, prompt caching, pricing and cost, authentication, cloud provider endpoints, websockets and secrets management.
- **Hands-on experience alone was not enough** for several Architect candidates; they paired building with reading the docs.
- **Exam-day logistics trip people**: the ID name must exactly match the registration.

## Anthropic's own prep prescription

The guide (Section 7) says no single course is required and no resource guarantees a pass. Its recommended combination:

1. Study the blueprint and self-assess against each objective by name. The objective pages here list the guide's wording and every sub-skill.
2. Review the official docs for the API, models, prompt engineering, Claude Code, Skills and MCP. Every objective page links them under Sources.
3. **Build and operate at least one Claude application** that uses the API, at least one tool, prompt and context engineering, and basic security and evaluation practice. The [labs](../labs/) exist for this.
4. Practise the competencies directly.
5. Work the sample questions to calibrate to item style.

## A plan for a 2 to 6 week window

**Week 1: the heavy half.** Claude Application Design, Software Engineering Foundations, API Mechanics, Technical Fundamentals and LLM Fundamentals. Run labs 01 and 02. One Quick 10 every day.

**Week 2: the rest of Domains 2 and 5.** Configuration Management, Requirements, Life Cycle, Model Selection, Cost. Run labs 04 and 06. First Mini mock at the end of the week.

**Week 3: agents, tools, prompting.** Domains 1, 6 and 8. Labs 03, 08 and 10. First full strict exam at the end of the week.

**Week 4 onward: security, Claude Code, and consolidation.** Domain 7 (lab 09), then Domains 3 and 4 quickly. Daily due reviews. A full strict exam every few days, reviewing every miss against its objective page rather than its answer key.

**Book the exam** when strict exams sit above 780 for two runs in a row and the readiness checklist below is complete.
