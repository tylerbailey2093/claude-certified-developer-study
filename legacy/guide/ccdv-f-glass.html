◆The strategy in three numbers

Domain 2 alone is 33.1%. Seventeen items. More than Claude Code, Eval/Debugging, Security, and Tools/MCP combined.

Domains 2 + 5 are 49.9%. Half the exam, and the half with the least carryover from Architect prep.

Domains 3 + 4 are 5.7%. Three items total. Learn the vocabulary and move on.

✓Scoring works in your favour

Scoring is criterion-referenced against a fixed standard, so there's no curve. Your report shows percent correct per domain, but those are diagnostic only and pass/fail comes from the total scaled score. There is no per-domain minimum, which is exactly why weighting your study time works: a strong Domain 2 can carry a weak Domain 3.

What the questions actually look like

From the official samples plus first-hand accounts of the sibling exams: stems run two to four sentences, four options, and three of them are engineered to look defensible. This isn't a documentation recall test. It asks whether you'd make the right call when a production system misbehaves.

✦The single highest-yield pattern

When a stem says something must or always happen, the answer is a deterministic control: a hook, a permission rule, a schema constraint. It is never a system-prompt instruction. A prompt is a request; only code is a guarantee. Candidates who passed the sibling exams all flag this same trap.

Where the prep course misleads you

The Academy prep course runs 774 minutes across five modules. Accelerators & IP Contribution takes 155 minutes, 20% of the runtime, for a sliver of blueprint coverage. Meanwhile MSO Foundations compresses Domain 5's 16.8% into 57 minutes. Course runtime and exam weight are close to inversely correlated at both ends.

If you fix only one thing

Go to Domain 2 → Claude Application Design. At 8.6% it is the heaviest single objective on the exam, worth more than Claude Code, Eval/Debugging, and Claude Hooks put together. It covers how instructions are interpreted across Claude Code, the API, Desktop, and claude.ai, plus content boundaries, schema design, and session hygiene. It is also the one objective the prep course never gives a dedicated lesson to, so most candidates arrive having never studied it directly.

What people who actually sat these exams report, and how to read the third-party market.

▲Evidence is thin, and you should know that

CCDV-F launched around 13 July 2026. Genuine first-hand accounts are scarce and no official pass rates exist for any Claude certification. Treat everything below as directional, not statistical.

What passers report

  • CCDV-F is rated the hardest of the four Claude certifications. It demands specific knowledge of the CLI, API, and Claude Code rather than general familiarity.
  • If you already build on the SDKs and API, you're most of the way there. The gap is memorising model configuration specifics you'd normally just look up.
  • The official Academy courses are described as very long and probably overkill for people already building.
  • On the sibling Architect exam, passers stress the same thing repeatedly: the deciding constraint is often buried mid-scenario. Read every stem twice.
  • Distractors are built so three of four options seem valid. Elimination beats recognition.
✕Avoid these providers

Any site advertising "real exam questions," "dumps," or "taken from actual exams" is claiming access to NDA-protected content. That's both a policy violation you agree not to engage with when you accept the exam NDA, and a reliability problem: the claim is almost certainly false. Named offenders in this space use exactly that phrasing.

Credible third-party material writes original questions mapped to the published blueprint and says so plainly.

Profile, prep, and policy

The parts of the guide that are not content but can still cost you the fee.

Are you the intended candidate?

The exam targets a hands-on builder who ships Claude applications, agents, and workflows. Recommended, not required:

  • One to five years of software engineering experience
  • At least six months hands-on with Claude or comparable LLM systems
  • Proficiency in Python and/or TypeScript
  • Fluency with REST APIs and CLI tools
  • Working understanding of LLM fundamentals, agents, context management, and MCP

Explicitly not the audience: non-technical users, anyone without hands-on development experience, and roles limited to prompt writing without broader application responsibility.

✓No prerequisites

There are no mandatory prerequisites or courses. The credential is awarded on exam performance alone, so the Academy path is optional however it is presented.

Anthropic's own prep prescription

Worth reading as a checklist, because it is the closest thing to an official answer on what preparation looks like. Note first that Anthropic states there is no single required course and does not guarantee any resource produces a pass. The recommended combination:

  1. Study the blueprint and self-assess against each objective by name
  2. Review official documentation for the API, models, prompt engineering, Claude Code, Skills, and MCP
  3. Build and operate at least one Claude application that exercises the API, integrates one or more tools, applies prompt and context engineering, and includes basic security and evaluation practice
  4. Practise the competencies directly: prompts and system instructions, agents and workflows, Claude Code configuration, token and cost management, guardrails, custom tools or an MCP server
  5. Work the sample questions to calibrate to item style
◆Item three is the one people skip

Anthropic puts build and operate one real application in its own bullet. Not read about, not watch. The hands-on tasks in the plan above exist to satisfy exactly this, and they are the difference between recognising an answer and knowing it.

Scoring, and why the weight strategy works

Criterion-referenced against a fixed standard set by a formal standard-setting study, so there is no curve and no competing against other candidates. Your report shows percent correct per domain, but those percentages are diagnostic only and pass/fail comes from the total scaled score. There is no per-domain minimum, which is precisely why weighting your study time by blueprint percentage works: a strong Domain 2 can carry a weak Domain 3.

◈Nothing here covers the heaviest objective

Claude Application Design is 8.6% and has no home module. Its pieces are scattered across modules 2, 3, and 4 and it never gets a dedicated lesson. Same for Understanding Requirements (3.4%). That is roughly 12% of the exam living in the seams between modules, which is what this guide exists to cover.

Week 1 · Foundations and the big domain

Watch: MSO Foundations again if it is already ticked complete. 57 minutes carrying 16.8% is the densest ratio in the course, and a single pass will not have held it.
Read: Domain 5 in full, then Domain 2 objectives 1–3.
Build: Run the basic Messages snippet from Domain 2. Iterate content blocks by type, print stop_reason, log every field of usage. Thirty minutes.

Week 2 · The rest of Domain 2

Watch: Production Engineering, Evals & Security (211 min). Full attention.
Read: Domain 2 objectives 4–6, plus Domain 7.
Build: Add a cache breakpoint to a large static system prompt. Run twice and watch cache_creation_input_tokens become cache_read_input_tokens. Then interpolate a timestamp into the prefix and watch the hit rate collapse to zero.

✦Why that one matters

Nearly every cost question on this exam resolves to understanding that prompt caching is an exact prefix match. Seeing your own hit rate die because of one interpolated value teaches it permanently in about four minutes. Reading this paragraph does not.

Week 3 · Agents, tools, prompting

Watch: Prompting, Agents & Tool Use (209 min) fast-pass. Then Claude Code, MCP & Integration (142 min): skim the Claude Code half, full attention on integration.
Read: Domains 1, 6, 8, 3.
Build: A three-request batch. Confirm for yourself that results join on custom_id and not on order. Then write a ten-line MCP server with one tool.

◆If you only run three

02, 06, and 09. Caching is the root of most cost questions, 06 exercises the heaviest objective on the exam, and 09 teaches the request-versus-control distinction that decides questions in three separate domains. Roughly seventy minutes for the best return available anywhere in this document.

Week 4 · Consolidation

Full Mock, timed, no notes. Review every miss against its objective rather than its answer key. Re-read the 400s checklist daily. Final pass over the official blueprint, self-assessing objective by objective.

✕Watch last, or not at all

Accelerators & IP Contribution, 155 minutes, 20% of the course runtime. Its framing is partner delivery: packaging a build so it survives reuse, review, and deployment past the engagement that produced it.

Correction on an earlier claim: this is not zero exam coverage. "Reuse" is the reasoning behind the custom-tool-versus-MCP-server decision in Agentic Customization (4.1%), and "review" touches code review in Software Engineering Foundations. But those ideas are covered faster and more directly elsewhere in this guide, and nothing else in the module is on the blueprint.

The real risk is the completion bar. Finishing five of five will feel like readiness while the heaviest objective on the exam remains unstudied.
